I wrote these guides so I would have one place to send people when they ask me how something works. Every chapter is built the same way, so once you know one, you know them all. This page is the key.

## Chapters

Each subject is a numbered chapter, in two parts: **Tech** (cloud, code, computers) and **Hands-on** (cars, tools, the house, survival). The number on the yellow tab at the top of a page is its chapter; the [contents page](/) lists them all, with how much of each is written and how much I have checked.

## Levels

Every chapter climbs the same six levels. The gauge next to a level name (▮▮▯▯▯) shows how far up the ladder a page sits.

| Level | Name | You can... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Orientation | Name the parts, tools, and vocabulary, and know the safety basics. |
| 1 | Beginner | Complete common jobs by following a walkthrough. |
| 2 | Intermediate | Do routine work on your own and spot mistakes. |
| 3 | Advanced | Take on multi-part jobs and diagnose problems from symptoms. |
| 4 | Expert | Do professional-grade work. |
| 5 | Mastery | Handle rare and complex work, and explain why things work the way they do. |

In the tech chapters, Level 5 goes down to internals and research papers. In the hands-on chapters, it means master-level craft, and no page here replaces the hours of practice that level takes.

## Walkthroughs

A walkthrough is one job, start to finish, graded **easy**, **moderate**, **hard**, or **expert** (brake pads are easy; a transmission rebuild is expert). Each one lists the time, tools, and parts it needs, then the steps, how to check the job worked, what to do when it didn't, and its sources. The jobs list on the [contents page](/) shows every walkthrough with its difficulty and status.

## Cheat sheets

Each chapter has one cheat sheet in three tiers:

- **Common:** what you'll use on most jobs.
- **Uncommon:** specific situations you'll meet eventually.
- **Rare:** recovery, niche, or risky operations. Every rare entry links to its official documentation.

Where a command differs between Windows and macOS, the tabs switch together across the whole page.

## Page status

Every page carries a stamp under its title, and the same status appears as a small tag wherever the page is listed. Anything short of checked also gets a warning slip at the top of the page. I set these by hand; nothing on this site marks itself checked.

| Stamp | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Planned | A placeholder. Nothing is written yet. |
| Draft | Written but not fact-checked. Don't rely on it. |
| In review | I am checking the sources against the text. |
| Checked | I checked every claim against the listed sources on the date on the stamp. |

The site will not build if a page is marked checked without a verification date and at least one source, so I cannot publish that promise by accident. The inspection log on the [contents page](/) shows how many pages are at each stage.

## Sources

Specifics — commands, flags, torque values, fluid types, part numbers, dosages, prices — come only from the sources listed at the bottom of a page, and I prefer the primary ones: official documentation, manufacturer service manuals, electrical codes, recognized first-aid curricula. Where I could not find a source, the page says so in a marked gap rather than guessing.

## Safety

Pages marked **safety-critical** cover work where a mistake can hurt someone: brakes, steering, fuel, lifting a vehicle, mains electricity, TV and microwave power supplies, gas, and medical care. They open with a warning, and their specs always come from the manufacturer's manual for your exact model; the page links to the manual rather than reproducing a "typical" number that may be wrong for yours.

## Corrections

If you spot an error or a missing source, use **Contact Us** at the foot of any page; it opens an issue on the site's repository. Corrections are the point of the stamps.
